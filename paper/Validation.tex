\section{Numerical Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{External Finite-Pulse Benchmark}
\label{sec:validation_smallwood}

We first validate the finite-pulse implementation against an
independent analytical result.
Smallwood \textit{et al.} derived perturbative third-order solutions of
the optical Bloch equations for finite-duration Gaussian pulses,
providing an analytical benchmark for two-dimensional coherent
spectroscopy beyond the impulsive limit
\cite{Smallwood2017FinitePulse}.
The same model was subsequently used by Rose and Krich to validate the
UF$^2$ finite-pulse algorithm
\cite{RoseKrich2021UF2}.

We reproduce this three-level rephasing two-dimensional photon-echo
benchmark using Model B of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}, since the analytical
treatment retains finite pulse duration while employing the
rotating-wave approximation.
The spectral coordinates are identified as
$\omega_1=\omega_\tau$ and $\omega_3=\omega_t$.
Because the two calculations employ different Fourier and response
normalizations, the analytical Smallwood result is converted to the
PULSUS convention through the fixed relation
%
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{PULSUS}}
=
-i\,2\pi\,S_{\mathrm{Smallwood}} .
\label{eq:smallwood_conversion}
\end{equation}
%
This factor follows entirely from the respective transform conventions
and is not fitted to the numerical result.

For the primary comparison, the waiting time is chosen as
$T=2\pi\sigma$, for which the pulses are sufficiently separated for
the factorization used in the PULSUS finite-pulse formulation.
Using the relative Frobenius discrepancy
%
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\mathrm{val}}
=
\frac{
\left\|
S_{\mathrm{num}}-S_{\mathrm{ana}}
\right\|_F
}{
\left\|
S_{\mathrm{ana}}
\right\|_F
},
\label{eq:validation_error}
\end{equation}
%
PULSUS agrees with the analytical spectrum to
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{val}}=7.33\times10^{-6}$,
corresponding to a relative discrepancy of $0.000733\%$.
For the same benchmark, the discretized UF$^2$ result used here differs
from the analytical spectrum by $0.343\%$.
The comparison is intended as an implementation and convention check,
rather than as a general accuracy ranking between the two numerical
methods.

Increasing the pulse separation to $T=8\sigma$ reduces the PULSUS
discrepancy to $1.70\times10^{-8}$.
The decrease is consistent with the remaining error at finite
separation arising from the separated-pulse time-ordering
approximation rather than from the Gaussian Liouvillian dressing
itself.

Fig.~\ref{fig:external_validation}.
