\begin{abstract}
We introduce PULSUS, a direct frequency-domain framework for
multidimensional spectroscopy of open quantum systems beyond the
impulsive rotating-wave approximation. Coherence-frequency intervals
are represented through Liouvillian resolvents, while finite Gaussian
pulses are incorporated through operator-valued functions of the
field-free Liouvillian. This enables direct evaluation on Cartesian
grids or arbitrary spectral coordinates while retaining molecular
evolution during the pulse envelopes and, when required, the complete
light--matter interaction.

The implementation is validated against analytical and independent
time-domain benchmarks and supports targeted spectral sampling that
reduces the number of evaluated coordinates when the response is
spectrally localized. A controlled hierarchy of finite-pulse,
rotating-wave, short-pulse, and impulsive models separates the errors
introduced by each approximation. For a coupled electronic dimer, the
total impulsive-RWA error is nonmonotonic in pulse duration: an
intermediate regime provides the highest accuracy, whereas ultrashort
pulses expose coupling-enabled beyond-RWA pathways and longer pulses
develop substantial finite-pulse corrections. These results show that
the impulsive and rotating-wave approximations cannot be assumed to
improve simultaneously and should be tested when quantitatively
interpreting multidimensional spectra. PULSUS provides a direct
frequency-domain framework for carrying out that comparison.
\end{abstract}
